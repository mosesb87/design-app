01 · Crawl

Read it like a machine

Sitemap, robots, canonicals, titles, schema and prices, pulled from the public HTML the way a crawler or an answer engine sees it. Nothing behind a login.

02 · Evidence

Date every claim

Every finding carries the date, the URL and the check behind it, so anyone can re-run it and see if it’s been fixed. Opinions don’t go in; observations do.

03 · Prototype

Build the fix

A scanner, a template, a rewritten page or a working browser. Something a team can use on Monday, not a list of recommendations.

04 · Plan

Sequence the work

A 30/60/90 plan that puts template fixes first, because one template change usually fixes hundreds of pages at once.

The playbook

36 checks,
six families

Not every check runs on every site. These are the ones that found the problems in the reviews on this site.

Pricing & promos6
  • Price on page vs cart
  • Bundle rules that conflict
  • Deal % named vs configured
  • Expired promos still live
  • Contest dates and rules
  • Volume ladders
Structured data6
  • JSON-LD on key templates
  • Product, FAQPage, Organization
  • LocalBusiness and store schema
  • GTIN and MPN in Product
  • Review counts that agree
  • llms.txt and AI signals
Indexing & crawl6
  • Canonicals to the right host
  • Sitemap returns XML
  • Staging or test pages indexed
  • Redirects left in sitemaps
  • Duplicate titles and descriptions
  • Confirmation pages indexable
Catalog data6
  • Encoding artifacts in names
  • Facet values split by spelling
  • Sold-out products still published
  • Duplicate listings and handles
  • Attributes leaking into titles
  • Identifiers and SKUs
Local & store data6
  • Name, address, phone agree
  • Hours across three sources
  • tel: links that work
  • Placeholder numbers indexed
  • Store pages that exist
  • Status: open vs coming soon
Launch & speed6
  • Domains connected
  • Password or parked pages
  • Analytics tags present
  • Mobile LCP and CLS
  • Help-center coverage
  • Go-live gate you can click
Anatomy

Five parts of
every finding

  1. 1Title

    A plain sentence that names the defect, not the category.

  2. 2Evidence

    Where it was seen and the exact values, copied from the page or schema.

  3. 3Date

    When it was observed, so it can be re-checked later.

  4. 4Impact

    Why a shopper, a crawler or the revenue team would care.

  5. 5Fix

    The smallest change that closes it, usually at the template.

Rules

Four rules
every review follows

01

Public pages only

No logins, no scraping behind walls, no private data. If a shopper or a crawler can’t see it, it isn’t in the review.

02

Template before page

One template fix usually beats a hundred page fixes. Findings are grouped by the template that causes them.

03

Numbers over adjectives

“43 sold-out products still published” instead of “inventory issues”. Every figure is counted, not estimated.

04

Dated and re-runnable

Each claim ships with the date and the check behind it, so it can be verified or closed later.

FAQs

About the reviews and how they’re made.

What is a review here?

A short, independent study of one company’s live website. I crawl the public pages, record every finding with its date and the URL or command behind it, and build a working fix: a scanner, a template, a rewritten page or a 90-day plan.

Did these companies hire you?

No. Each review is unsolicited and made from public pages only. No logins, no private data, and no affiliation with the companies named.

How long does one take?

Most are built in one to two days. The crawl and evidence come first; the prototype and the plan come after the findings are checked.

Why is every figure dated?

Websites change. Every number carries the date it was observed, so anyone can re-check it and a fixed issue is easy to tell apart from an open one.

Can you review my site?

Yes. Send the URL and what the site needs to do better. The first findings come back dated and sourced, with one working fix.
