The report · four parts

The storefront, in brief.

The same shape I'd use for a weekly note to the Director of E-Commerce: what's working, what needs attention, why it matters, and what I'd do first.

i.

What's working

The migration landed: 816 titles were created in April and new releases have been added every month since, with ISBN SKUs, book-detail metafields, an Authors filter and Shop Pay Installments. Shopify's agent endpoints are already on.

ii.

What needs attention

Some data came over from Magento with gaps: two prices for the same book across Hay House sites, duplicate listings, filters that split formats and authors, zero weights, templated alt text and 5,623 tags.

iii.

Why it matters

Filters, Google Shopping, social catalogs, shipping rates and AI shopping agents all read the same product data. Clean it once and every channel improves at the same time.

iv.

What I'd do first

Week one: agree the price of record, merge duplicates, fix the Format and Authors filters, add weights. Then H1s and book structured data with the developers, tags into metafields, and alt text in batches.

ISBNs as SKUs

All 878 variants use an ISBN-13 as the SKU, and every one passes the check-digit test. That makes matching, feeds and redirects much easier.

Book details

Product pages already show pages, language, publication date, ISBN and publisher from metafields, and the Authors filter is live.

Agent-ready plumbing

Shopify publishes /agents.md, an agentic discovery sitemap and a UCP profile (2026-08-25) for the store, so AI shopping agents can search the catalog and start checkouts that the buyer approves.

A reader who arrives on the Shopify page from Google, Instagram or an AI assistant can pay nearly twice what hayhouse.com charges for the same ISBN. Shoppers and price-comparing agents will find both pages.

Agree which storefront is the price of record and sync the other from it, through a scheduled price sync or one product feed. From the postingCreate and maintain SOPs and process documentation

Three SOPs I'd write in the first month, each with an owner, a trigger, steps with the system used, and a QA list. Each one fixes a finding at the source, so the problem doesn't come back with the next import.

SOP-01

New title setup, from metadata to live

Owner · Sr. E-Commerce Specialist

Steps

  1. Check the ISBN-13 check digit and confirm no product already uses it. catalog check
  2. Create one product per ISBN. Use the title as published, with the edition in the title when two editions share a name. Pick the format from the controlled list: Paperback, Hardcover, Card Deck, Journal, Calendar, Boxed Set. Products
  3. Fill the metafields: authors (Author entries), subtitle, pages, language, publication date, publisher, BISAC subjects, trim size. Metafields
  4. Set SKU and barcode to the ISBN, enter the price, leave compare-at empty, and enter the weight. Variant
  5. Upload the cover first (at least 1,600 px on the long side), then interior pages, and write alt text for each image. Media
  6. Write the description (sentence case, author bio, contents if available), SEO title and meta description. SEO
  7. Add to topic collections and New Releases; link other editions and formats. Collections
  8. Schedule for the on-sale date and set up pre-order if needed. Status
  9. QA on a phone, run the catalog check and the Rich Results test, and confirm the Google and Meta channels approve the product. QA

Mapping rules

  1. Match on the ISBN from the Magento export, never on the URL text. 1,271 legacy slugs end in -1, -2 and so on, and "ask-and-it-is-given-2" is the deck, not the book.

  3. Ebooks, audio and courses stay where they're sold today.
  4. One hop only: no chains from old path to root URL to Shopify.
  5. No match means a decision, not a redirect to the home page.

Image alt text is a good first job for ChatGPT Enterprise. In a sample of 220 product images, 211 had templated alt text ("Title - image 3"), 9 repeated the title, and none described the image. Here's the workflow I'd run, with the human checks built in.

  1. 1 · ExportProducts, formats and image URLs from the Admin API, 50 products per batch.
  2. 2 · PromptChatGPT Enterprise gets the image plus title, author, format and image position. Nothing else.
  3. 3 · ValidateA script checks length, the exact title and author, banned phrases and the JSON shape.
  4. 4 · ReviewA person reads every result in the first 50, then 1 in 10. Flagged items always get a review.
  5. 5 · ImportApproved text goes back through the Admin API or the bulk editor.
  6. 6 · LogPrompt version, reviewer, date, before and after. Any change can be rolled back.

Prompt

You write alt text for product images on shop.hayhouse.com.
Input: one image, plus TITLE, AUTHOR, FORMAT and POSITION.

Rules:
- One sentence, 125 characters or fewer.
- POSITION 1 is the cover: start with "Cover of {TITLE} by {AUTHOR}",
  then say what the cover shows.
- Other positions: say what the image shows (an interior page,
  sample cards, the box) and its main subject.
- Use TITLE and AUTHOR exactly as given.
- No claims, prices, awards or health benefits.
- Don't start with "Image of" or "Picture of". No emoji, no ALL CAPS.

Return JSON only: {"alt": "...", "confidence": "high" | "low"}
