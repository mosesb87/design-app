Deals experience + operational layer

LIV built the store. The deals never reached the door.

02 — What I observed

Click “Deals” anywhere on the site. You never arrive at a deal.

Every deals affordance on livcannabis.com routes somewhere that isn't deals — a locations list, or a store's generic home page. Meanwhile a real, working specials page sits on the new platform, unlinked from the site that's supposed to send people to it.

04 — Why it matters

Deals are the most perishable content a dispensary has.

A price, a window, an eligible brand, a participating store — all of it changes constantly, and all of it has to stay identical across the web store, the marketing site and two apps. Here's what the observed gap puts at risk.

01

Deal-seekers hit a wall

The most commercially-intent click on the site — “Deals” — leads to a locations page or a generic storefront. The one page built to convert them is the one they can't find.

02

Drift is the default

With two front-ends and two apps and no single source of truth, a promotion is correct in one place and stale in another. The six-vs-seven store mismatch is that drift, already visible.

03

Trust leaks at the edges

A 2022-era “we're transitioning to a new site” stub still greets shoppers at some locations in 2026. First impressions like that are hard to win back.

04

Every launch is manual risk

Without validation and a publish step, catching an expired date, a missing exclusion or an empty store list depends on someone remembering to look.

What I'm not claiming: I have no access to LIV's analytics, so I make no claim about lost revenue, conversion rate or abandonment. Those require internal data. Everything above is a UX and operational consequence inferred from the observable experience — and clearly labelled as such.

05 — The solution

Two sides of one product.

Not a portfolio site and an unrelated dashboard — a single system. What a customer sees, and how the organisation decides what they see. One is impossible to keep honest without the other.

Customer side

A deals front door

Location-aware deal cards with eligibility, exclusions, participating stores, live expiry and an honest “auto-applied at checkout” signal — then a clean handoff to the menu.

one source
Operations side

A Deal Operating System

Create → validate → QA → approve → publish. Every deal is checked before it can go live, and it publishes to web and both apps from one record.

08 — How the system prevents the problem

A deal only reaches a customer if it earns it.

Watch one deal move through the pipeline. A clean deal validates, passes QA, gets approved, and publishes into the customer front door. A broken one — the exact kind of gap I documented — is stopped at the gate and never reaches a customer.
