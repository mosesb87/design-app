## Lens: Sapience AI Head of Brand & Communications / Head of Marketing Communications. Judged on coherence with the cream, serif and purple identity while the icon refresh runs in parallel; clarity for association executives and board members; honest claims and correct use of the research §11 copy; readiness to launch by Dec 9; and how well the candidate understands the company.

**Winner:** proceedings

I would hire the Proceedings candidate, and I would send that concept to the Seattle room. STRATA is the most dazzling piece of creative work and the deepest reading of our language. If this were a motion-portfolio jury, it would win. But under a brand and comms lens, three things count against it. Its central image, drilling and extracting cores from members' knowledge, is an extraction metaphor for an AI company whose buyers' top barrier is privacy. Its Bodoni shift and nineteenth-century survey world would become the brand's permanent costume just as our own refresh lands. And its 1,800vh 3D descent asks too much of board members. Wayfinding explains the mechanism most clearly (the sign-wall alignment is excellent), but it moves the identity furthest: grotesk headlines and body, new track colours, and a pictogram system that overlaps the brand designer's scope. It also puts a waitlisted Chrome capability in the hero as our headline promise.

The Annotated Record is the only concept that treats our identity as something to steward rather than replace. Cream, purple as ink, and an editorial serif each get a defined job, and the wordmark is kept deliberately generic until the icon refresh ships. Its honesty is built into the product rather than bolted on: the Provenance view, the cite buttons, the flagged claims left out, and 'capability to be confirmed' where it applies. That is exactly the discipline a comms lead needs from the person who will own our site through launch and maintenance.

It is also the most legible option for executives and boards: a readable document with contents, chapters that map to their objections, print, and a mode that says 'with motion off, the record reads the same'. It carries a story sales can repeat, and it has the lowest build risk against Dec 9. Its weaknesses can all be fixed without breaking its core rule ('print is set once, ink is live'): a quiet first viewport, hairline motion that may vanish on a projector, a flagged 57% set as the hero stat, and a missing proof slot and launch-page templates. The grafts below come mostly from STRATA's signature ideas, translated into the book's ink grammar.

### strata

**Strengths**
- The closest reading of the company of the three. The metaphor comes from Sapience's own words (Private Intelligence Core, 'the layer', 'sit above', 'built to compound', 'it gets smarter every year', 'The Organization That Never Forgets'), so the concept adds no new metaphor, and the brief says so.
- Its H1, 'The organization that never forgets.', is the most memorable and repeatable line in the set. It is footnoted correctly as the title of Sapience's proposed SXSW27 session, per §11.2.
- Industry data is handled cleanly. It uses only attributed industry readings (iMIS/ASI 43%, ASAE/Avenue M 92% vs 6–13%) and avoids the unconfirmed 57% and $150K–$300K figures. Both flagged claims ('You own the model.', 'No third-party vendor processes your member records.') are left out, and there are no certifications. Every scenario is marked Illustrative, and a 'Field reports' proof slot is reserved.
- The fifth compartment of the core tray is a strong signature moment. It is left empty, with a dashed outline and a trail to the 2018 gap, and it shows 'a person, not a prompt' rather than saying it. It also draws on the ASAE finding that the reasoning behind a decision is not documented.
- Every set piece maps to a published mechanism: the datum and tie lines for organize and connect, choosing where to drill for Sage's routing, the log sheet for the audit trail, the cased bores for per-tenant isolation.
- The system shows the most craft of the three: neatline, plate captions, legend, marginalia, a title-block footer and a mono Labs register. Its art pipeline is deterministic and build-time, and it renders a finished 'Still plate edition' that also prints.
- It shows awareness of the brand context. The logo is a labelled slot, it plans a Libre Baskerville fallback token, and it anticipates the Sage name collision.

**Weaknesses**
- The drill-and-extract image works against the brand. For an AI company whose buyers name privacy as their top barrier, 'drilling' cores out of members' contributions reads as data extraction. That undercuts 'That knowledge belongs to the people who created it.' One of the eases is even named 'extract'. A vocabulary rule cannot change what the picture says.
- Bodoni Moda is a Didone with fashion and luxury associations and very thin hairlines. That is a bigger tonal move away from the Baskerville-like serif than a journal serif would be, and it is being made while the Brand Graphic Designer is refreshing the guidelines. The hairlines are also fragile on projectors in the Seattle review room.
- It is hard work for board readers. The page runs about 1,800vh, with about 730vh pinned, a 3D descent, and terms like unconformity, hiatus, datum and casing (glossed, but still a load). Multi-generational readers will get lost before they reach governance.
- It overclaims capability. The climax line 'a person you can ask' and the log row 'Person suggested · Chair…' present people-routing as a platform capability, but the published platform routes to agents only, and Chrome peer-matching is on a waitlist. The only label is 'Illustrative'. There is no capability flag.
- It is a thematic world, not a style. After launch, every Platform, Company, Careers or launch-announcement page has to become a 'plate' in a nineteenth-century geological survey, which is costly for content contributors to maintain and for the refreshed brand to live inside.
- The build is the riskiest of the three. About 30 CSS-3D SVG faces, Flip inside scrub, MorphSVG and resize handling in the middle of a pin are all estimated at 13 plus 6 days, which is optimistic for Dec 9.

**Fatal flaws (as judged)**
- Potentially fatal for comms unless reframed: the central image, a core drilled and extracted from members' knowledge, is an extraction metaphor for an AI vendor selling to privacy-anxious boards.

### proceedings

**Strengths**
- The best brand coherence. It keeps the brand's own palette, and every colour gets a job: charcoal is the record, purple is the editor's ink, and purple is never glow, gradient or field. Newsreader keeps the editorial serif register. The wordmark is deliberately set as a plain caps slot 'so the concept does not copy the logotype', which is exactly right while the icon refresh is running.
- It occupies the whitespace the research names (§5.7.4, 5 and 6) in a way association executives recognise from their own world: proceedings, journals, standards, minutes, running heads, an index.
- It has the strongest honesty system of the three. A Provenance view tags every block as verbatim, concept, illustrative or industry source, with 2px versus dashed rules. That is an audit tool I would use myself. There is a 'cite' button that copies the quote with its source URL. Flagged claims are left out. The unverified person-suggestion capability is named as a risk and tagged 'capability to be confirmed'. SEO and JSON-LD sit behind a production flag to avoid adding to the entity collision.
- It is the clearest for boards. It reads as a document: contents, chapters that map to buyer objections (problem, integration, answer, agents, continuity, governance), a print stylesheet, and the line 'With motion off, the record reads the same.'
- The signature sequence explains the mechanism without spectacle. An answer is typeset from five marginal sources, each shifting from italic margin to roman text as it lands, and the fifth source is a person in a double frame. The worked CE-credit example uses real association vocabulary (hybrid annual meeting, continuing-education credit, chapters, renewal).
- The motion principle is disciplined and defensible: print is set once, ink is live. The planning is honest too. The candidate checked what the fonts actually contain, budgets 20–22 days, and gives the form an honest 'Concept only. This form isn't connected.' message with its HubSpot hand-off documented.
- Its sales line is repeatable in one breath: 'Your members' knowledge lives in the margins. Sapience sets it in the main text, with the trail.'

**Weaknesses**
- The first viewport is the live site's H1 plus one hairline leader, so it risks reading as 'our current site with footnotes'. It has no launch-worthy line or visual hook for the fall product launch.
- It is the quietest of the three on motion. Much of the motion is 1px hairlines, which can vanish on the projector in weekly reviews.
- It sets 57% as a display stat of up to 10rem when the figure is flagged 'to be confirmed'. It also is not a strict complement of iMIS's 'Only 43% can easily access and understand'. A verified number should carry the page instead.
- It has no explicit honest proof slot for customer stories (research §10.7). 'Provenance as proof' is a good idea, but the CEO will still ask where design-partner stories go.
- The launch scope is thin. It covers the index, /security, /notes and a 404, with no templates yet for Platform, Company, Customers, Labs, Developers, Contact or Careers, and no announcement slot (§3.4 #12).
- It moves the body text to a serif while the brand refresh is still in progress. The body face is a single token, but the decision is not yet coordinated with the Brand Graphic Designer.
- '[Description forthcoming]' for Affinity and Corporate implies a commitment to publish, and 'Sage reads' underlining key terms implies parsing that no published source describes.
- In Ch VI the shadow deepens during the animation, which contradicts its own rule that only transform, opacity and clip-path animate.

### wayfinding

**Strengths**
- The Sage mapping is the most exact of the three. The information desk carries Sage's verbatim description, and 'without knowing which tool to use' is exactly what a desk does for a visitor.
- Its signature moment is the most legible for executives. Twenty-four contradicting signs click into alignment and fuse into one line with the stops Organize · Connect · Activate. It explains 'organize, connect, activate' and 'No rip and replace' in about four seconds.
- It draws on association vernacular: annual-meeting badges with ribbons, the program at a glance, committee chairs, recertification. Executives will recognise it immediately.
- It handles people-routing most honestly of the three, in room 05. The status plate says that, as published, Sage routes to agents, and that finding the person is what the Chrome extension is working toward, in early access with a waitlist. Affinity and Corporate read 'Description to be published by Sapience AI'. Stories are 'To be announced' slots.
- The system thinking speaks to naming and taxonomy architecture. Codes, tracks and sign components can take renamed products without a redesign. Presenter mode and skip plates are well suited to the Seattle reviews.
- Making the serif the 'human' voice is an argued choice rather than an accident.

**Weaknesses**
- It departs most from the current identity. A grotesk becomes the system voice for the H2s and body text. The concept adds green and brass track colours, uses large purple sign panels as fields, and introduces a 26-glyph pictogram set that overlaps the Brand Graphic Designer's scope for the icon and supporting assets.
- The hero promise, 'The person who knows →', repurposes the Chrome extension's waitlisted tagline as the company's headline promise in the first viewport. It is not flagged until room 05.
- The sign wall aligns 'Past chairs' email', 'Desktop folders', 'Listserv threads' and similar into Sapience's trunk. The caption says these are illustrative, but the motion says Sapience ingests staff email and desktops, which is a privacy implication buyers will notice.
- It uses the flagged $150K–$300K figure as row S3 under 'Community Stories', where an unsourced ROI number sits in the proof area. It also uses iMIS's 46% win-back figure to suggest people-routing, which stretches that statistic.
- The transit and signage metaphor is common in tech (journey maps, subway diagrams) and can drift toward kiosk or airport clip-art. 'Lost & Found' is flippant for executives.
- The estimate of 11 dev-days plus 3 design-days is optimistic for 26 pictograms, a computed route builder, a 300vw pinned corridor and a MorphSVG, with about 820vh pinned in total.

**Fatal flaws (as judged)**
- Seen through this lens, the hero turns a waitlisted Labs capability into the company's headline promise in the first viewport, and it is not qualified until five rooms later.

## Lens: Awwwards / FWA jury creative director: originality of the core idea, art direction, typography, motion choreography and pacing (impact → calm → discovery → pause → acceleration → resolution), memorability of the signature moment, coherence of the evolving motif, and avoidance of AI-website clichés. Checked against research sections 3, 5.5–5.7, 10 and 11.

**Winner:** strata

STRATA wins because it is the only concept with all three things a jury rewards: a core idea that belongs to the client, authored art direction, and a signature that is an image rather than an effect.

- The metaphor is not imposed. Sapience already calls its product a 'Core', itself 'the layer', and its thesis 'The Organization That Never Forgets'. STRATA prints that vocabulary as a survey plate.
- The art direction (engraved lithology, neatline, cartouche, Bodoni, flat washes) has a real lineage and extends cream, serif and purple instead of replacing them. It avoids every §5.5 cliché.
- The empty fifth compartment, a dashed box with a hairline running to the 2018 gap, is the one frame across all three concepts that a hiring panel will describe to each other afterwards.
- The motion has laws (gravity, engraving, only the answer rises), not a catalogue of tricks.
- The rail mini-column that becomes the title-block key column is the most coherent evolving motif submitted.
- The concept already absorbs most of PROCEEDINGS' editorial register (footnotes, marginalia, plates, Fig. captions), so it wins that territory with far more impact.

PROCEEDINGS is the more refined and safer piece, and its 'print is set once, ink is live' rule is better than anything in STRATA's colour logic. But it has no impact beat, and it will read as tasteful footnoting. WAYFINDING has the single most legible mechanism beat (THE ALIGNMENT), but its visual language is borrowed from transit signage, its climax comes in chapter 2, it demotes the brand's serif, and its hero leans on an unpublished capability.

STRATA's problems are real but fixable:
- The drill/extract contradiction with its own 'Nothing to excavate'.
- A Flip that visually merges the client's systems.
- Excessive length and post-climax sag.
- Apparatus overload.
- An H1 without the category.

None of these is a flaw in the idea itself. They can all be fixed by editing, mostly by grafting the losers' best disciplines onto it.

### strata

**Strengths**
- The metaphor comes from Sapience's own words taken literally: Private Intelligence Core, 'the layer', 'sits above', 'built to compound', 'The Organization That Never Forgets'. Nothing is imported from outside. 'Geologists call a missing layer an unconformity. Associations call it a handover.' is the best line of copy in all three concepts.
- It has real art direction with a named lineage: William Smith's 1815 map, nineteenth-century survey plates, lithology hatching, neatline and cartouche. It carries cream, serif and purple into print. None of the §5.5 AI-uniform tropes appear.
- The signature is an image, not an effect. A tray holds four cited segments and an empty fifth compartment, with one hairline running back to the 2018 gap. It makes the case for 'a person, not a prompt' without saying it. Visitors will remember it.
- The motion has a physical grammar, not a list of effects: things arrive under gravity, lines are engraved, and only the answer rises. The descent covers 30 years in 140vh and the ascent covers them in 60vh, so time has weight. This is real choreography.
- It has the strongest evolving motif of the three. The mini stratigraphic column in the rail gains state on every plate (hiatus glyphs, ties, core, LOGGED stamp, casing, new layers) and then Flips out of the rail into the title block as the key column.
- Mobile is redesigned, not shrunk: a portrait phone becomes a borehole log with a 40px spine. The Still plate edition works for reduced motion, no-JS and print at once, which suits board readers.
- It covers whitespace items #2, #3, #4, #5 and #6 of §5.7 at the same time. The honesty handling is careful: flagged sentences are left out, industry figures are attributed, and illustrative content is labelled.

**Weaknesses**
- It contradicts itself. Plate V's headline is 'Nothing to excavate', and Plate VI's signature act drills and extracts a core, with an ease literally named 'extract'. Mining members' knowledge also sits badly with 'That knowledge belongs to the people who created it.'
- In Plate VI, Flip merges the four system columns into 'one full-width section'. Visually that says 'we consolidated your systems', which undercuts 'sits above' and 'No rip and replace'.
- Exploded isometric slabs are close to the B2B 'platform stack' diagram cliché, the same stacked-layer graphic every data-infrastructure company uses.
- It is too long: about 1800vh with about 730vh pinned. After the Plate VI climax, four more plates (VII–X) follow before the resolution, so the second half sags. The Core log table in VI duplicates the log sheet in VII.
- There is too much apparatus: neatline ticks, depth rail, mini-column, plate numerals, imprint, plate caption, marginalia, cartouche and legend all compete. Fussiness is the main art-direction risk.
- The jargon load (unconformity, hiatus, datum, lithology, correlation, casing) is heavy for multi-generational, non-technical executive readers.
- The H1 is the proposed SXSW session title, not the category. That is weak for SEO and entity disambiguation (§3.4 #6, §10.13), and the 'proposed' status is fragile.
- Showing Sage's routing as 'choosing where to drill' is a loose mapping. Sage routes questions to agents, not to locations in the data.
- Purple ink is used for all of the record's linework, so colour does not separate what the community already has from what Sapience adds.
- Formation labels set on textPath inside rotated CSS 3D faces will render soft. Bodoni Moda hairlines at small optical sizes are risky on projectors in the Seattle review room.
- CSS 3D with 30 hatched SVG faces, plus Flip inside a scrub, carries real performance risk on Safari, integrated GPUs and 1× projectors.

### proceedings

**Strengths**
- 'Print is set once, ink is live' is the most disciplined motion rule in the set. Colour and motion carry the same meaning: charcoal is the record and purple is Sapience's action.
- The signature does real typographic work. An italic margin clone rides a leader and becomes roman body text as it lands, so the register change is the moment knowledge enters the record.
- The typographic details are at jury level. The sidenote grows into the epigraph with an opsz 16→72 tween inside nowrap spans, so nothing reflows, and the ¹ morphs into a hanging quotation mark.
- The finale is elegant: 'together' rides in from the margin and turns from purple into charcoal, and its leader keeps going to become the CTA underline. The motif resolves into the conversion ask.
- Provenance view and the 'cite' button build honesty into the UI. The brand and comms leads can audit every word.
- It is the most feasible and lowest-risk concept, with a text-first LCP. The font-feature constraints were checked, and the reduced-motion edition reads the same as the full one.
- Contents, running heads and an index make it very easy to review in a room.

**Weaknesses**
- There is no impact beat. The opening is one hairline leader, and the pacing arc starts at 'impact (quiet)', which is a contradiction.
- Tufte sidenotes and Distill-style marginalia are a well-known web pattern. The concept risks reading as 'the current site with footnotes', as it admits itself.
- The signature is intellectual rather than visual. An executive will struggle to recall it as a single image a week later.
- Setting '57%' at 10rem is the big-stat trope, even with a 'to be confirmed' flag.
- It breaks its own flatness rules: box shadows on the vellum and slips, 'shadow deepens' on the binding, and drift in Chapter I.
- Leader geometry is computed at runtime from DOM rectangles. It is fragile under zoom, font loading and projector resolutions, and a misaligned hairline looks broken.
- The tail after the climax is long (Chapters IV–VI, Appendix, Afterword, back matter), so the pacing sags after Chapter III.
- Newsreader is increasingly common on editorial and AI sites, and the serif body text departs from Sapience's sans body.

**Fatal flaws (as judged)**
- For a motion-led pitch judged at Awwwards level, it has no memorable visual signature and no true impact beat. The restraint is likely to read as absence to the CEO and go-to-market people in the room.

### wayfinding

**Strengths**
- THE ALIGNMENT is the most instantly legible mechanism image in all three concepts. Twenty-four contradicting signs snap to one direction in a diagonal wave and fuse into one trunk carrying Organize, Connect and Activate. A non-technical executive gets it in about four seconds.
- Motion snaps in discrete 45° clicks. It is a crisp, recognisable grammar that sets the concept apart from smooth-everything scroll sites.
- 'The route was the audit trail' is a real narrative twist, and it puts governance on the main path.
- It uses the association's own vernacular: badges with ribbons, the session board, the information desk. The audience will recognise their world, and no competitor uses it.
- Honest proof slots appear as 'To be announced' sessions with dashed future stations. That convention is honest and elegant.
- It is very reviewable: presenter mode, skip plates, a Directory dialog and aria-current on the route.
- Fitting sign lines to their plates with the width axis is real typographic craft, and 'wayfinding as taxonomy made visible' speaks to the brand lead's naming remit.

**Weaknesses**
- Its visual lineage (Vignelli, Kinneir Calvert, tube-map diagrams) is borrowed wholesale. The line that draws itself as you scroll down the page is one of the most overused scrollytelling devices.
- The climax comes in room 02, and the eight rooms after it fall in intensity, so the pacing is front-loaded and the rest feels anticlimactic.
- A 200vh pinned horizontal corridor is both a scroll-jacking cliché and a trackpad and resize hazard.
- A set of 26 bespoke pictograms risks clip-art, and municipal-utility styling can read as less premium than an editorial register.
- The pendulum 'hang' ease and arrows that point at the cursor are cute rather than meaningful.
- 'Lost & Found' is flippant for executives, and the 11-dev-day estimate is unrealistic for ten rooms, a corridor, a route builder under resize and a MorphSVG room.

**Fatal flaws (as judged)**
- The hero promise, 'The person who knows →', rests on a capability Sapience has not published. Sage routes to agents, and Chrome peer-matching is waitlisted. The headline idea itself depends on a flagged claim, against the spirit of §10.1 and §10.8.
- It departs from the brand. A grotesk-led system voice, a serif demoted to a secondary voice, and added green and brass track colours with large purple sign fields go against §10.4 ('keep the chosen identity and go editorial'). The Head of Brand is the wrong person to show that to.

### Grafts proposed
- From PROCEEDINGS and WAYFINDING (both arrived at it independently), make colour carry meaning. Draw the record (strata boundaries, hatching, washes) in charcoal and graphite, and reserve --ink purple only for what Sapience adds: the datum, correlation ties, the core line, provenance trails, citation superscripts and the CTA. Every purple mark then means 'Sapience did this', which also sharpens 'sits above'.
- From PROCEEDINGS, 'references move, data doesn't'. The core does not remove rock. It is a purple-ink survey reading. The bands stay intact, with no hole left behind, and the tray holds ink impressions of each segment, each tied back by its trail. This removes the extraction connotation and the clash with 'Nothing to excavate'.

### strata

**Strengths**

**Weaknesses**

- Detail A tweens the viewBox of an SVG full of micro-text. Text re-layout and re-raster happen every frame of the scrub, which is heavy on integrated GPUs.
- The 768–1199 'tablet' layout tier and the 900px motion split don't line up. At 768–899 the tablet grid meets the mobile motion branch, which doubles the QA matrix.

**Fatal flaws (as judged)**
- The 'one continuous take' can't be built as specified. The stage is CSS-sticky for Plates I–IV (a 500vh wrapper), then handed to four separate GSAP pins (V, VI, VIII, IX), and it returns for the ascent and the outro. One DOM stage can't be both. The build needs duplicated stages with disguised hand-offs, and the concept doesn't specify them. Every hand-off is a visible seam or a pop in forward and reverse scrub.
- CSS 3D of about 30 inline-SVG faces with 4px hatch patterns under scrubbed rotateX/Y plus scale and a 1100px translate. With will-change, Chrome rasterizes at the starting scale, so the hatching goes soft. Without it, the faces re-raster every frame and jank. Hatching under rotation shimmers and moirés, and Safari's preserve-3d handling of SVG children is prone to flattening, z-fighting and seam cracks. The concept's own .lite fallback admits the risk. This is exactly how a site looks unfinished in a review.
- Brand connotation: drilling cores out of members' knowledge reads as data extraction. That undercuts Sapience's value 'That knowledge belongs to the people who created it', and a vocabulary rule in the copy deck can't fully neutralize an image.
